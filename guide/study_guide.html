Rule for every answer
Address the member who asked: "First Sergeant, ..." or "Sergeant Major, ...". Give the answer, then the reference if you know it. If you don't know, say "First Sergeant, I do not know the answer, but I will find out." Never guess a number or paragraph. A confident wrong answer hurts you more than an honest "I'll find out."

Creeds & Army Values

Your board opens with a creed. Get it perfect, because it sets the tone for everything after.
Verify word for word
The texts below are the standard published versions. Before you lock them in, compare every word against army.mil/values/nco.html and army.mil/values/soldiers.html. Most boards ask for the NCO Creed. The MOI will say which one.
Must know  The Creed of the Noncommissioned Officer

No one is more professional than I. I am a noncommissioned officer, a leader of Soldiers. As a noncommissioned officer, I realize that I am a member of a time honored corps, which is known as "The Backbone of the Army". I am proud of the Corps of noncommissioned officers and will at all times conduct myself so as to bring credit upon the Corps, the military service and my country regardless of the situation in which I find myself. I will not use my grade or position to attain pleasure, profit, or personal safety.

Competence is my watchword. My two basic responsibilities will always be uppermost in my mind—accomplishment of my mission and the welfare of my Soldiers. I will strive to remain technically and tactically proficient. I am aware of my role as a noncommissioned officer. I will fulfill my responsibilities inherent in that role. All Soldiers are entitled to outstanding leadership; I will provide that leadership. I know my Soldiers and I will always place their needs above my own. I will communicate consistently with my Soldiers and never leave them uninformed. I will be fair and impartial when recommending both rewards and punishment.

Officers of my unit will have maximum time to accomplish their duties; they will not have to accomplish mine. I will earn their respect and confidence as well as that of my Soldiers. I will be loyal to those with whom I serve; seniors, peers, and subordinates alike. I will exercise initiative by taking appropriate action in the absence of orders. I will not compromise my integrity, nor my moral courage. I will not forget, nor will I allow my comrades to forget that we are professionals, noncommissioned officers, leaders!

How to lock it in
Each paragraph starts with its own anchor: Professional → Competence → Officers. Recite it out loud 3 times a day, standing at attention, ideally in front of someone who can follow along with the text. Speak at a steady pace. If you stumble, pause, then keep going. Don't start over.

Board Procedure & AGSU

Your appearance and how you report in are graded before you answer a single question.
Local check: the MOI decides
Below is a common battalion-board sequence. Some details vary by unit: whether you salute an NCO board president, how many knocks, and the exact reporting phrase. Ask your 1SG or a recent board-goer, and follow the MOI once it comes out.

Typical reporting sequence

[Knock once, firmly. Wait.]
CSM: "Enter."
[Enter. Close the door. March by the most direct route to about 2 paces in front of the president, centered. Halt.]
YOU: "Sergeant Major, Specialist ________ reports to the President of the Board." [Salute if the MOI says to. Hold it until it's returned or you're told to drop it.]
CSM: "Recite the NCO Creed." [Stand at attention. Speak up and keep your eyes forward.]
CSM: "Take your seat." [Sit straight, back off the chair back, feet flat, hands palms down on your thighs.]
CSM: "Tell the board about yourself." [See Section 4.]
[Each 1SG asks questions. Address each one as "First Sergeant."]
CSM: "Stand up. You are dismissed." [Stand at attention. Salute if required. Face about. Exit and close the door.]

Bearing

  • Eye contact with whoever asked the question.
  • No fidgeting, "um," or "like." Pause instead.
  • Speak a little louder and slower than feels natural.
  • If you didn't hear the question: "First Sergeant, could you repeat the question?"
  • If you make a mistake, correct it calmly: "Correction, First Sergeant..."

What graders usually score

  • Military bearing and self-confidence
  • Personal appearance and uniform
  • Oral expression and conversational skill
  • Knowledge of world affairs Local
  • Awareness of programs and leadership
  • Basic Soldiering knowledge
Confirm the actual scoresheet in the MOI.

NCO History, Duties & Personal Questions

These are where the board decides whether you're ready to lead. Prepare your answers. Don't wing them.

"Tell the board about yourself" (60–90 seconds)

Structure

  1. Name, rank, MOS, unit, position. "Specialist ____, 11B, Team Leader in 2nd Platoon, C Company, 2-12 Infantry."
  2. Hometown and time in service.
  3. Your career so far: M320 grenadier, Stryker driver, SAW gunner and rifleman, now team leader.
  4. One or two accomplishments: schools, ranges, rotations (NTC, the border mission), awards.
  5. Goals: BLC, civilian education, next position.
  6. Close: "...and that is a little about me, Sergeant Major."
Leave out family drama, politics, and anything you can't defend.

NCO history & duties

Know
The NCO Corps dates to 1775 with the Continental Army. Baron von Steuben's "Blue Book" (Regulations for the Order and Discipline of the Troops of the United States, 1778–79) first defined NCO duties in writing.
Know
The first Sergeant Major of the Army was SMA William O. Wooldridge (1966).
Bonus
The NCO Creed was written in the early 1970s at Fort Benning, as part of the NCO education system. Verify
Know
TC 7-22.7, The Noncommissioned Officer Guide (19 Aug 2025), is the NCO's reference for duties, responsibilities, and authority. TC 7-21.13, The Soldier's Guide (17 Sep 2025), is the Soldier-level reference.
Know
NCOs carry out the commander's intent. They lead Soldiers, train individuals and teams, maintain standards and discipline, and take care of Soldiers and their equipment.
Follow-ups the 1SGs ask New

SMA William O. Wooldridge, 1966.
What is the "Backbone of the Army"?
The NCO Corps.
What is the NCO Guide?
TC 7-22.7, The Noncommissioned Officer Guide.
Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
A realistic goal: squad leader and SSG, ALC complete, college progress, and developing Soldiers.
What makes a good team leader?
Leading by example, knowing your Soldiers, enforcing standards consistently, and training your team to be ready to take your job.

Leadership & Counseling

As a team leader, counseling is what you do every day. Expect scenario questions.
Must know
Army leadership is the activity of influencing people by providing purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization. (ADP 6-22)

Must know Attributes (what a leader IS)

CCharacterPPresenceIIntellect
Character: Army Values, empathy, Warrior Ethos/service ethos, discipline, humility. Presence: military and professional bearing, fitness, confidence, resilience. Intellect: mental agility, sound judgment, innovation, interpersonal tact, expertise. Verify ADP 6-22 Ch 2–4

Must know Competencies (what a leader DOES)

LLeadsDDevelopsAAchieves
Leads: others, builds trust, extends influence, leads by example, communicates. Develops: creates a positive environment, prepares self, develops others, stewards the profession. Achieves: gets results. Verify ADP 6-22 Ch 5–7

Counseling: ATP 6-22.1 (13 Feb 2024)

Must know
Three categories: Performance, Professional Growth, Event. (Ch 2, para 2-4)
Must know
DA Form 4856, Developmental Counseling Form, documents counseling.
Know
Event counseling examples: specific instances (good or bad), reception and integration, crisis, referral, promotion, separation. Verify
Know
Three approaches: Nondirective, Directive, Combined. Verify
Know
Four-stage counseling process: 1) Identify the need for counseling 2) Prepare 3) Conduct the session 4) Follow up. Verify
Know
Conducting the session: open the session, discuss the issues, develop a plan of action, record and close. Verify
Bonus
Counselor qualities: respect, self-awareness and cultural awareness, credibility, empathy. Verify
Know
Local: the 4ID Yellow Book (19 Feb 2026) is the local counseling SOP. It supplements Army doctrine and does not replace it.

Evaluations: AR 623-3 (14 Feb 2025)

Know
DA Form 2166-9-1 = NCOER for SGT. DA Form 2166-9-1A = NCOER Support Form. Neither one is a counseling form. DA Pam 623-3 is the procedural pamphlet.
Board answers
Define leadership.
Influencing people by providing purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization.
Name the three leader attributes and the three competencies.
Attributes: Character, Presence, Intellect. Competencies: Leads, Develops, Achieves.
What are the three categories of counseling?
Performance, professional growth, and event.
What form is used for counseling? For a SGT's NCOER?
DA Form 4856. DA Form 2166-9-1.
When should you do an initial counseling?
When a Soldier first arrives (reception and integration), and within the first 30 days of a rating period. Verify AR 623-3
Follow-ups the 1SGs ask New
What are the levels of leadership?
Direct, organizational, and strategic. A team leader works at the direct level. Verify ADP 6-22.
What's the difference between counseling, coaching and mentoring?
Counseling: a leader and subordinate review performance and potential. Coaching: guiding someone to improve a specific skill. Mentoring: a voluntary, long-term relationship between someone more experienced and someone less experienced, for development.
What should a counseling plan of action include?
Specific actions, who is responsible, and a deadline, so the Soldier knows exactly what to do to improve.
What is the NCOER rating chain for a SGT?
Rater, senior rater, and supplementary reviewer, if one applies. Verify AR 623-3.
What does it mean to lead by example?
Meeting every standard you enforce: PT, appearance, timeliness. Soldiers do what they see.

Situational Questions

"Specialist, you're a team leader and..." There's no single right answer. The board wants to see you think like an NCO.
The 5-part answer
1. Safety first: is anyone in danger?  ·  2. Take immediate action: stop it, correct it, or secure it.  ·  3. Find the facts: talk to the Soldier.  ·  4. Use the right resource or regulation, and inform your chain.  ·  5. Document and follow up (DA Form 4856).
Speak in the first person and in the order you'd act: "First Sergeant, first I would..., then I would..."

Discipline & standards

SituationA strong answer
Your Soldier is late to formation for the second time.Talk to them privately to find out why. Do event counseling on a DA Form 4856 with a plan of action. Apply corrective training if appropriate. Inform your squad leader. Follow up. If it keeps happening, it moves up the chain.
A Soldier from another unit walks past you out of uniform standard.Make an on-the-spot correction, professionally and respectfully. Every NCO enforces standards, not just the ones in their own unit.
Your Soldier failed the AFT.Find out why: injury, preparation, or effort. Build a plan with them (H2F resources, extra PT). Counsel and document. Follow your chain's policy on flags and retests.
Your Soldier failed the ABCP waist-to-height check.Make sure the assessment followed AD 2026-13 (a second team confirmed it, recorded on DA Form 5500). Counsel the Soldier. Connect them with H2F dietitians and trainers. Inform your chain. Be supportive and hold the standard.
You smell alcohol on your Soldier at first formation.Remove them from duty and from any weapon or vehicle. Inform your squad leader or PSG right away. The command decides on testing. ASAP referral (AR 600-85). Document it.
Your Soldier posted a photo of the vehicle line and the deployment date on social media.Have them take it down right away. Explain the OPSEC risk (AR 530-1). Inform your chain, since it may need to be reported. Counsel and retrain.

Soldier care

SituationA strong answer
Your Soldier says, "Sometimes I don't see the point of anything anymore."ACE. Ask directly: "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" Care: listen and take it seriously. Escort: stay with them and take them to Behavioral Health, the chaplain or the ER. Never leave them alone. Inform your chain. If there's immediate danger, call 911.
A Soldier tells you they were sexually assaulted.Make sure they're safe and get medical care if needed. Don't promise confidentiality. Tell them you have to report it, and connect them with the SARC or SHARP VA right away. The VA explains restricted and unrestricted reporting. Don't investigate yourself. Protect their privacy. AR 600-52.
Your Soldier is being harassed by another Soldier because of their race.Stop the behavior. Make sure the victim is safe. Inform your chain. Explain the EO complaint options: informal, or formal on DA Form 7279. Connect them with the unit EOL. Protect them from retaliation. AR 600-20 Ch 6.
Your Soldier can't pay rent this month.Find out the cause. AER for emergency assistance. ACS financial readiness counseling. Help them build a budget. Follow up next payday.
Your Soldier's family member is in the hospital back home.Inform your chain right away for emergency leave. Red Cross message. AER for travel funds if needed. Check on them while they're gone.
A new Soldier just arrived on your team.Sponsor them. Do the initial counseling within the first 30 days. Learn their background, goals and family situation. Get them through in-processing, issue and training. Pair them with a battle buddy.

Leadership & mission

SituationA strong answer
You get an order you think is unsafe.Bring your concern to the leader who gave it, respectfully, with facts and risk-management steps. If it's lawful, execute it with controls in place. If it's clearly illegal, do not obey, and report it through the chain.
Your squad leader is gone and the PSG gives your team a mission.Acknowledge it and back-brief. Use the TLPs: issue a WARNORD, plan, inspect (PCCs/PCIs), execute. Report completion. "Initiative by taking appropriate action in the absence of orders." (NCO Creed)
Two of your Soldiers keep arguing and it's affecting the team.Separate them and talk to each one individually. Find the root cause. Mediate. Set clear expectations. Counsel if needed. Inform your squad leader if it keeps happening.
Your team is behind on PMCS before a field problem.Prioritize: get the vehicle and weapons mission-capable first. Divide the tasks by who knows what. Inform the chain about deadline faults early, not at the last minute. Do an AAR afterward so it doesn't happen again.
Your friend from your old team is now your Soldier.Have the conversation early. Explain the relationship has changed professionally. Enforce the same standard for everyone. Avoid any appearance of favoritism (AR 600-20 / AR 600-32).
You made a mistake that affected the mission.Own it right away. Tell your leader what happened and what you're doing to fix it. Learn from it in the AAR. Integrity matters more than looking good.

Your team makes contact on a patrol.Return fire and take cover. Locate the enemy. Send a SALUTE report. Gain fire superiority. Follow the battle drill (react to contact) and the squad leader's direction. Send a LACE report after contact.
A Soldier on your team is wounded under fire.Care Under Fire: win the fight first. Direct the casualty to self-aid and cover. Once it's safe, apply a tourniquet if there's massive bleeding. Move to Tactical Field Care (MARCH). Request MEDEVAC with a 9-line.
You find a suspected IED on your route.Stop. Don't touch it. Confirm from a distance. Clear the area. Cordon it off. Check for secondary devices. Control the area. Report with a 9-line UXO/IED report. Verify the 5 Cs: Confirm, Clear, Call, Cordon, Control.
