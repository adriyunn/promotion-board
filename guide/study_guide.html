Rule for every answer
Address the member who asked: "First Sergeant, ..." or "Sergeant Major, ...". Give the answer, then the reference if you know it. If you don't know, say "First Sergeant, I do not know the answer, but I will find out." Never guess a number or paragraph. A confident wrong answer hurts you more than an honest "I'll find out."

Creeds & Army Values

Your board opens with a creed. Get it perfect, because it sets the tone for everything after.
Verify word for word
The texts below are the standard published versions. Before you lock them in, compare every word against army.mil/values/nco.html and army.mil/values/soldiers.html. Most boards ask for the NCO Creed. The MOI will say which one.
Must know  The Creed of the Noncommissioned Officer

No one is more professional than I. I am a noncommissioned officer, a leader of Soldiers. As a noncommissioned officer, I realize that I am a member of a time honored corps, which is known as "The Backbone of the Army". I am proud of the Corps of noncommissioned officers and will at all times conduct myself so as to bring credit upon the Corps, the military service and my country regardless of the situation in which I find myself. I will not use my grade or position to attain pleasure, profit, or personal safety.

Competence is my watchword. My two basic responsibilities will always be uppermost in my mind—accomplishment of my mission and the welfare of my Soldiers. I will strive to remain technically and tactically proficient. I am aware of my role as a noncommissioned officer. I will fulfill my responsibilities inherent in that role. All Soldiers are entitled to outstanding leadership; I will provide that leadership. I know my Soldiers and I will always place their needs above my own. I will communicate consistently with my Soldiers and never leave them uninformed. I will be fair and impartial when recommending both rewards and punishment.

Officers of my unit will have maximum time to accomplish their duties; they will not have to accomplish mine. I will earn their respect and confidence as well as that of my Soldiers. I will be loyal to those with whom I serve; seniors, peers, and subordinates alike. I will exercise initiative by taking appropriate action in the absence of orders. I will not compromise my integrity, nor my moral courage. I will not forget, nor will I allow my comrades to forget that we are professionals, noncommissioned officers, leaders!

How to lock it in
Each paragraph starts with its own anchor: Professional → Competence → Officers. Recite it out loud 3 times a day, standing at attention, ideally in front of someone who can follow along with the text. Speak at a steady pace. If you stumble, pause, then keep going. Don't start over.

Board Procedure & AGSU

Your appearance and how you report in are graded before you answer a single question.
Local check: the MOI decides
Below is a common battalion-board sequence. Some details vary by unit: whether you salute an NCO board president, how many knocks, and the exact reporting phrase. Ask your 1SG or a recent board-goer, and follow the MOI once it comes out.

Typical reporting sequence

[Knock once, firmly. Wait.]
CSM: "Enter."
[Enter. Close the door. March by the most direct route to about 2 paces in front of the president, centered. Halt.]
YOU: "Sergeant Major, Specialist ________ reports to the President of the Board." [Salute if the MOI says to. Hold it until it's returned or you're told to drop it.]
CSM: "Recite the NCO Creed." [Stand at attention. Speak up and keep your eyes forward.]
CSM: "Take your seat." [Sit straight, back off the chair back, feet flat, hands palms down on your thighs.]
CSM: "Tell the board about yourself." [See Section 4.]
[Each 1SG asks questions. Address each one as "First Sergeant."]
CSM: "Stand up. You are dismissed." [Stand at attention. Salute if required. Face about. Exit and close the door.]

Bearing

  • Eye contact with whoever asked the question.
  • No fidgeting, "um," or "like." Pause instead.
  • Speak a little louder and slower than feels natural.
  • If you didn't hear the question: "First Sergeant, could you repeat the question?"
  • If you make a mistake, correct it calmly: "Correction, First Sergeant..."

What graders usually score

  • Military bearing and self-confidence
  • Personal appearance and uniform
  • Oral expression and conversational skill
  • Knowledge of world affairs Local
  • Awareness of programs and leadership
  • Basic Soldiering knowledge
Confirm the actual scoresheet in the MOI.

NCO History, Duties & Personal Questions

These are where the board decides whether you're ready to lead. Prepare your answers. Don't wing them.

"Tell the board about yourself" (60–90 seconds)

Structure

  1. Name, rank, MOS, unit, position. "Specialist ____, 11B, Team Leader in 2nd Platoon, C Company, 2-12 Infantry."
  2. Hometown and time in service.
  3. Your career so far: M320 grenadier, Stryker driver, SAW gunner and rifleman, now team leader.
  4. One or two accomplishments: schools, ranges, rotations (NTC, the border mission), awards.
  5. Goals: BLC, civilian education, next position.
  6. Close: "...and that is a little about me, Sergeant Major."
Leave out family drama, politics, and anything you can't defend.

NCO history & duties

Know
The NCO Corps dates to 1775 with the Continental Army. Baron von Steuben's "Blue Book" (Regulations for the Order and Discipline of the Troops of the United States, 1778–79) first defined NCO duties in writing.
Know
The first Sergeant Major of the Army was SMA William O. Wooldridge (1966).
Bonus
The NCO Creed was written in the early 1970s at Fort Benning, as part of the NCO education system. Verify
Know
TC 7-22.7, The Noncommissioned Officer Guide (19 Aug 2025), is the NCO's reference for duties, responsibilities, and authority. TC 7-21.13, The Soldier's Guide (17 Sep 2025), is the Soldier-level reference.
Know
NCOs carry out the commander's intent. They lead Soldiers, train individuals and teams, maintain standards and discipline, and take care of Soldiers and their equipment.

As a team leader, counseling is what you do every day. Expect scenario questions.
Must know
Army leadership is the activity of influencing people by providing purpose, direction, and motivation to accomplish the mission and improve the organization. (ADP 6-22)

Must know Attributes (what a leader IS)

CCharacterPPresenceIIntellect
Character: Army Values, empathy, Warrior Ethos/service ethos, discipline, humility. Presence: military and professional bearing, fitness, confidence, resilience. Intellect: mental agility, sound judgment, innovation, interpersonal tact, expertise. Verify ADP 6-22 Ch 2–4

Must know Competencies (what a leader DOES)

LLeadsDDevelopsAAchieves
Leads: others, builds trust, extends influence, leads by example, communicates. Develops: creates a positive environment, prepares self, develops others, stewards the profession. Achieves: gets results. Verify ADP 6-22 Ch 5–7

SHARP, EO & Suicide Prevention

Every board asks at least one. Precision matters: don't mix up SHARP, EO and harassment reporting.

SHARP: AR 600-52 (11 Feb 2025)

Must know
Ch 2 = sexual harassment. Ch 3 = sexual assault. Ch 9 = retaliation. Para 3-3 = restricted and unrestricted reporting.

Restricted report

Confidential. No command notification of the victim's identity and no investigation. The victim gets medical care, advocacy, and counseling. Some exceptions apply. Made to a SARC, a SHARP VA, or healthcare personnel. Verify the list in para 3-3.

Unrestricted report

Triggers command notification and an investigation. The victim also gets medical care, advocacy, and counseling. It can be made through the chain of command, law enforcement, a SARC, or a VA.
As a leader, never do this
Never promise a Soldier confidentiality yourself. You are not a restricted-reporting channel. Connect them to a SARC or SHARP Victim Advocate, who can explain both options and the exceptions.
Must know
DA Form 7746 = Sexual Harassment Complaint (Jan 2026). DA Form 7279 = Equal Opportunity and Harassment Complaint (Jul 2020). These are separate processes from each other and from sexual-assault reporting.

Equal Opportunity: AR 600-20, Chapter 6

Know
A formal EO complaint uses DA Form 7279. Complaints can be informal or formal. Verify the current 2026 chapter text before you quote filing deadlines, protected categories, or timelines.

Suicide prevention: AR 600-92 (4 Oct 2024)

AAsk your buddy directlyCCare: listen, take it seriouslyEEscort to help. Don't leave them.
Must know
If a Soldier may be at immediate risk, stay with them, call for emergency help (911, or 988 then press 1 for the Military Crisis Line), and escort them to qualified care: Behavioral Health, the ER, or the chaplain.
Board answers

AR 600-52.

What form records a sexual harassment complaint?
DA Form 7746.

DA Form 7279.

Promotions, Awards & ABCP

You're at a promotion board. Know the process you're in.

Promotions: AR 600-8-19 (6 Mar 2026)

Must know
Chapter 3 covers semi-centralized promotions (SGT and SSG). Para 3-8 covers unit promotion boards. Para 3-12 covers board proceedings.
Know
SGT eligibility per the HQDA cutoff-score memo (Feb 2026). Primary zone: 34 months TIS / 10 months TIG. Secondary zone: 18 months TIS / 6 months TIG. The 2026 revision removed waivers for secondary-zone requirements. Verify AR 600-8-19 Table 3-1.
Know

Bonus
Promotion points fall into categories: military training (weapons qualification, fitness), awards and decorations, military education, and civilian education. Check your points in IPPS-A. Verify category maximums.
Know
AR 600-8-22, Military Awards (11 May 2026). Don't quote approval authorities from 2025 guidance.

Army Body Composition Program: Army Directive 2026-13

The 2026 rule: waist-to-height only
Waist circumference (at the navel) ÷ height (inches). Truncate after 3 decimals. Don't round.
Less than 0.550 = PASS.   0.550 or more = FAIL.
A second, different team confirms an initial failure the same duty day. Record it on DA Form 5500. DA Form 5501 is no longer used. A high AFT score does not exempt you.
ExampleMathResult
34.5 in waist, 70 in tall34.5 ÷ 70 = 0.4928…→ 0.492PASS
38.5 in waist, 70 in tall38.5 ÷ 70 = 0.5500 → 0.550FAIL
Board answers

What is the ABCP pass standard?
Waist divided by height must be less than 0.550. 0.550 fails. (AD 2026-13)
Does a high AFT score exempt a Soldier from ABCP?
No.
What form records the body composition assessment?
DA Form 5500.
