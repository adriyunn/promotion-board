Situational Questions

"Specialist, you're a team leader and..." There's no single right answer. The board wants to see you think like an NCO.
The 5-part answer
1. Safety first: is anyone in danger?  ·  2. Take immediate action: stop it, correct it, or secure it.  ·  3. Find the facts: talk to the Soldier.  ·  4. Use the right resource or regulation, and inform your chain.  ·  5. Document and follow up (DA Form 4856).
Speak in the first person and in the order you'd act: "First Sergeant, first I would..., then I would..."

Discipline & standards

SituationA strong answer
Your Soldier is late to formation for the second time.Talk to them privately to find out why. Do event counseling on a DA Form 4856 with a plan of action. Apply corrective training if appropriate. Inform your squad leader. Follow up. If it keeps happening, it moves up the chain.
A Soldier from another unit walks past you out of uniform standard.Make an on-the-spot correction, professionally and respectfully. Every NCO enforces standards, not just the ones in their own unit.
Your Soldier failed the AFT.Find out why: injury, preparation, or effort. Build a plan with them (H2F resources, extra PT). Counsel and document. Follow your chain's policy on flags and retests.
Your Soldier failed the ABCP waist-to-height check.Make sure the assessment followed AD 2026-13 (a second team confirmed it, recorded on DA Form 5500). Counsel the Soldier. Connect them with H2F dietitians and trainers. Inform your chain. Be supportive and hold the standard.
You smell alcohol on your Soldier at first formation.Remove them from duty and from any weapon or vehicle. Inform your squad leader or PSG right away. The command decides on testing. ASAP referral (AR 600-85). Document it.
Your Soldier posted a photo of the vehicle line and the deployment date on social media.Have them take it down right away. Explain the OPSEC risk (AR 530-1). Inform your chain, since it may need to be reported. Counsel and retrain.

Soldier care

SituationA strong answer
Your Soldier says, "Sometimes I don't see the point of anything anymore."ACE. Ask directly: "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" Care: listen and take it seriously. Escort: stay with them and take them to Behavioral Health, the chaplain or the ER. Never leave them alone. Inform your chain. If there's immediate danger, call 911.
A Soldier tells you they were sexually assaulted.Make sure they're safe and get medical care if needed. Don't promise confidentiality. Tell them you have to report it, and connect them with the SARC or SHARP VA right away. The VA explains restricted and unrestricted reporting. Don't investigate yourself. Protect their privacy. AR 600-52.
Your Soldier is being harassed by another Soldier because of their race.Stop the behavior. Make sure the victim is safe. Inform your chain. Explain the EO complaint options: informal, or formal on DA Form 7279. Connect them with the unit EOL. Protect them from retaliation. AR 600-20 Ch 6.
Your Soldier can't pay rent this month.Find out the cause. AER for emergency assistance. ACS financial readiness counseling. Help them build a budget. Follow up next payday.
Your Soldier's family member is in the hospital back home.Inform your chain right away for emergency leave. Red Cross message. AER for travel funds if needed. Check on them while they're gone.
A new Soldier just arrived on your team.Sponsor them. Do the initial counseling within the first 30 days. Learn their background, goals and family situation. Get them through in-processing, issue and training. Pair them with a battle buddy.

Leadership & mission

SituationA strong answer
You get an order you think is unsafe.Bring your concern to the leader who gave it, respectfully, with facts and risk-management steps. If it's lawful, execute it with controls in place. If it's clearly illegal, do not obey, and report it through the chain.
Your squad leader is gone and the PSG gives your team a mission.Acknowledge it and back-brief. Use the TLPs: issue a WARNORD, plan, inspect (PCCs/PCIs), execute. Report completion. "Initiative by taking appropriate action in the absence of orders." (NCO Creed)
Two of your Soldiers keep arguing and it's affecting the team.Separate them and talk to each one individually. Find the root cause. Mediate. Set clear expectations. Counsel if needed. Inform your squad leader if it keeps happening.
Your team is behind on PMCS before a field problem.Prioritize: get the vehicle and weapons mission-capable first. Divide the tasks by who knows what. Inform the chain about deadline faults early, not at the last minute. Do an AAR afterward so it doesn't happen again.
Your friend from your old team is now your Soldier.Have the conversation early. Explain the relationship has changed professionally. Enforce the same standard for everyone. Avoid any appearance of favoritism (AR 600-20 / AR 600-32).
You made a mistake that affected the mission.Own it right away. Tell your leader what happened and what you're doing to fix it. Learn from it in the AAR. Integrity matters more than looking good.

Tactical

SituationA strong answer
Your team makes contact on a patrol.Return fire and take cover. Locate the enemy. Send a SALUTE report. Gain fire superiority. Follow the battle drill (react to contact) and the squad leader's direction. Send a LACE report after contact.
A Soldier on your team is wounded under fire.Care Under Fire: win the fight first. Direct the casualty to self-aid and cover. Once it's safe, apply a tourniquet if there's massive bleeding. Move to Tactical Field Care (MARCH). Request MEDEVAC with a 9-line.
You find a suspected IED on your route.Stop. Don't touch it. Confirm from a distance. Clear the area. Cordon it off. Check for secondary devices. Control the area. Report with a 9-line UXO/IED report. Verify the 5 Cs: Confirm, Clear, Call, Cordon, Control.
